Job Openings & Labor Turnover

How many jobs are open, how many people move into them, and how many leave — the flows behind the payroll count.

Openings are a stock measured on the last business day; hires, quits and layoffs are flows across the whole month.

Table 3Vacancies per unemployed personRatio, s.a.

Openings divided by the number of unemployed. Above one, there are more vacancies than jobseekers — the tightness measure the Fed watches.

Table 4Beveridge curveRates, s.a.

The vacancy rate against the unemployment rate, traced in time order. Movement along the curve is the cycle; a shift of the whole curve is a change in how well the labour market matches people to jobs.

Table 5Hires against total separationsPercent of employment, s.a.

Employment grows only while hires exceed separations. The gap between these two lines is the payroll change.

Table 6Separations, in fullMillions, s.a.

Quits and layoffs are the two everyone watches, but they do not add up to separations on their own. Other separations — retirements, transfers between locations, deaths — runs at a few hundred thousand a month and is what closes the total. Reading quits and layoffs alone against hires therefore leaves a gap that looks like a data problem and is not.

Table 7Does JOLTS agree with the payroll survey?Thousands per month, 12-month average

Hires less total separations is what JOLTS says the month’s net employment change was; the establishment survey counts it directly. Two independent measures of one quantity, and they agree on average — the mean gap over 308 months is about 4,000 a month on series that move by hundreds of thousands. Month to month they do not agree at all: the standard deviation of the gap is roughly 936,000, so both sides are shown as twelve-month averages. This is a consistency check over a year, never a read on a single month. Analyst-derived.
