Table 10Months distinguishable from zeroPercent of the trailing 24 months

The release states a 90% confidence interval of ±122,000 on the monthly change. This is the share of the last twenty-four months whose published change exceeded it — how often the headline is large enough to mean anything at all. Roughly 70% of months since 1990 clear that bar. Analyst-derived.

Table 11Breakeven employment growthThousands per month, s.a.

Unemployment holds steady when employment grows with the labour force, so the gain required is the labour force’s own growth net of the share that stays unemployed — an accounting identity, not a forecast. The labour force is shrinking, so breakeven has fallen through zero and a negative payroll print can still sit above it. Two windows are drawn because the estimate is sensitive to how far back it looks, the monthly change carrying sampling error of several hundred thousand; it is negative on both. January is excluded as a population-control level break rather than a flow, and the line breaks where the October 2025 household survey was not collected. Breakeven is on the household concept while payrolls count jobs, so the comparison is indicative. Analyst-derived.

Table 12Why the employment ratio movedPoints, change over 12 months

The employment-population ratio is participation multiplied by the share of the labour force in work, so its twelve-month change separates exactly into the two. Through the 2021–22 recovery the move came mostly from unemployment falling; the decline since is almost entirely participation, which makes it demographic and behavioural rather than a sign of rising joblessness. The gap between the ink line and the two components is the interaction between them. Analyst-derived.
