Weekly Claims

The highest-frequency read on the labour market. Published every Thursday, five weeks ahead of the payroll report that covers the same period.

Table 1Initial claimsPersons, s.a.

New applications for unemployment insurance. Weekly data is noisy, so the four-week average is the series to read.

Weekly4-week average

Table 2Continuing claimsPersons, s.a.

People still claiming after the first week. Rising continuing claims against flat initial claims means the newly unemployed are taking longer to find work.

Table 3Insured unemployment ratePercent, s.a.

Continuing claims as a share of covered employment — the claims equivalent of the unemployment rate, and unaffected by changes in labour force participation.

Table 4What seasonal adjustment removesPersons, 3 years

The same series adjusted and unadjusted. January spikes above 300,000 every year without meaning anything; this is what the adjustment is for, and why the raw number is never the story.

Claims lead payrolls. Both indexed so the turning points line up rather than the levels. Payrolls are monthly on a weekly axis, so its line is drawn through the intervening weeks.
