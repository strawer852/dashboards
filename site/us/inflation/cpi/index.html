Table 10What makes up the CPI, latest monthPercentage points of the 12-month change

Not inflation rates — contributions: each item’s own twelve-month change multiplied by its share of the basket, so the four add up to the headline rather than merely sitting beside it. Table 20The same items, over four yearsPercentage points, 48 months

The ranking in Table 17 is one column of this. Contributions rather than rates, so cells are directly comparable across items: a dark cell is a large push on the index, not merely a large price move on something small. Four years reaches back into the 2022 peak, where gasoline and used cars ran dark and shelter had not yet taken over. October 2025 is the blank column. Only new vehicles, used cars and gasoline were priced that month, so only those three carry a contribution through it. A near-white cell anywhere else is a small contribution, not a missing one: the hole is the column blank in every other row.

Table 25Vehicles and travel, yearPercent, year over year, s.a.

Lines, not a stack. These five are a selection, not a decomposition — stacking them would draw a total that does not exist, and two of them sit inside transportation services rather than beside it, which is exactly why they are absent from the single-item contribution charts above. Motor vehicle insurance is the striking one: 2.6% of the basket and falling outright at -4.5% over the year, having risen at double digits for most of the preceding three. It is published by BLS but not carried by FRED as a seasonally adjusted series, so it arrives through the BLS adapter with no revision history (trap 15). Two of the three series that survived October 2025 are here — new and used vehicles; the third was gasoline.

Table 30What seasonal adjustment removesPercent, month on month

The same index twice, as monthly changes: the published unadjusted series and its seasonally adjusted form. Over twelve months the two are nearly identical — seasonality cancels — so every other chart here can use either. Month to month they are not remotely the same, and the unadjusted line is the one that would have you reading January as a shock every year. The only panel on the page carrying an unadjusted series.

Table 31What revises, and what does notIndex points, first published to latest

Payroll figures are revised because more survey responses arrive. The CPI is not revised at all in that sense — the published index is final the day it prints. The unadjusted line here has not moved on a single one of the 66 months since 2021; it sits exactly on zero, which is the point of drawing it. What does move is the seasonally adjusted index, on 59 of those same 66 months, because BLS recalculates seasonal factors each January across the prior five years. So an SA revision is a change to the adjustment, never to the price data underneath — and this year’s months read zero until next January reaches back and touches them.
