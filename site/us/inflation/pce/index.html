PCE

Consumer prices as the Federal Reserve measures them, and the income and spending they are computed from. The 2% target is written in this index and not in the CPI, and the two disagree by enough to matter.

Table 3PCE against CPITwelve-month change, %

The same country, two numbers. PCE reweights every quarter from what people actually bought where the CPI reweights every two years from a survey, and PCE also counts what employers and government spend on a household’s behalf — chiefly medical care. PCE runs below CPI nearly always, which is why a 2% target is not a 2% CPI.

Table 12What the typical price is doingTwelve-month change, %

Headline and core are weighted means, and a mean is what a few violent items carry. This is the median: half the basket by expenditure share rising faster, half slower. Core removes food and energy by assumption; the median sets an item aside only for being at the edge this month. Built on a 210-item partition from BEA’s underlying detail — FRED carries 25 of those lines — whose weights sum to 99.7. Analyst-derived, and BEA-sourced, so it carries no revision history even though the headline above it does.
